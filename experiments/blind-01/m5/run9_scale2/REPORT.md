## Inferences, conditional on recorded protocol

**Reduced scale makes threshold pass weakly harder (`harder`).** Let `S` be reduced set of poses and `T` its expanded counterpart retaining same poses and per-case settings. Then `S ⊆ T` implies `max DockQ(S) ≤ max DockQ(T)`. With fixed lower cut-off 0.23, adding opportunities cannot turn passing maximum into failure; removing opportunities can remove every passing pose. Smaller scale therefore cannot make this gate easier under that comparison. “Harder” encodes this direction, not a demonstrated strictly lower pass probability. No independence or identical-distribution assumption needed. Measured run already passes; nested expansion preserving its winner would also pass. Additional cases could leave maximum unchanged. Actual reruns changing prior poses fall outside this deterministic nesting argument.

Measured maximum can depend on scale while published **criterion** stays scale-free. These statements concern different objects and do not conflict. No evidence that intake compensated for smaller maximum by lowering threshold.

**Best description: `consistency_check`.** Gate establishes existence of at least one acceptable pose among selected candidates, consistent with brief's pose-quality criterion. It does not constrain output near 0.23: all valid maxima at or above threshold satisfy comparison. Published empirical baseline, uncertainty, equality tolerance, repeated-run variance and perturbation sensitivity are not established. Consequently “main quantity agrees with published value at a known precision” is unsupported. Ordinary usage of “small calibration experiment” appears in brief, but cannot supply missing numerical calibration evidence.

Gate has structural blind spots: ranking changes preserving poses, failures confined to unselected cases, and degradation of nonwinning jobs can leave pass unchanged. Fifteen selected jobs currently contain passing candidates. Those observations justify limited scope, not a probability over plausible alternative outputs. They do not establish question 6's stronger `weak_check` definition, “would pass for most plausible outputs.” Among offered descriptions, consistency check fits established evidence best, interpreted as consistency with a published criterion rather than equality to published performance.

## Unknowns

- Actual expected maximum, variance, and pass-probability difference between reduced and published scales; no stochastic model or full-scale run established.
- Whole-pipeline repeatability and sampling noise. `intake/benchmark.json:scoring_and_repeatability.determinism` says not established; DockQ evaluation deterministic conditional on fixed decoys. `EXECUTION.json:timing_scope.repeats` says each job run once. Eight sorted cases are not independent repeated experiments.
- Numerical sensitivity to scientifically material errors, probability most plausible outputs pass, and effect of controlled wrong-data/parameter/software perturbations. No such controls established in reviewed evidence. Large threshold surplus alone settles none of these.
- Published FroDock performance at full scale, numerical agreement with that performance, and precision of such agreement. Missing tolerance is not zero tolerance; classification threshold is not uncertainty estimate.
- Identity of mirrored FroDock bytes with original distribution. Brief's materials section retains `unverified_mirror` and explicitly states anchor agreement does not prove identity. Successful local execution does not remove this uncertainty.

These unknowns limit claims; they do not erase observed execution, acceptable recovered poses or exact dataset counts. Status values encode six requested classifications only.
