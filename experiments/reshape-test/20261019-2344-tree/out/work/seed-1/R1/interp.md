# Mech-interp (Goodfire-style) solutions to time-parallelising a long sequential simulation

## 0. Where the lens applies (and where it does not)

The framer's objects — a Metropolis–Hastings sampler and a lumped rainfall-runoff model stepped through decades of daily rainfall — are not neural networks, so Goodfire-style mechanistic interpretability (sparse autoencoders trained on neural activations, plus feature steering) has nothing to operate on *there*: an SAE decomposes a neural activation vector into a sparse linear combination of learned "feature directions" (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features; https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/), and there is no such activation vector inside GR4J or a Metropolis step.

But the framer's abstraction — *one long sequential trajectory `x_{t+1} = f(x_t)`, guess the whole thing and correct every step at once, which pays only when the system forgets its past* — has a natural neural instantiation: an autoregressive language model's residual stream evolving token-by-token and layer-by-layer is exactly a long sequential simulation. There, Goodfire-style tools apply, and the question "are there solutions?" gets four concrete answers below. The honest headline is that mech interp is a *measurement and correction* toolkit, not itself a parallelisation algorithm: it does not remove the step-after-step dependency the framer describes, but it supplies the two things the framer's own analysis says are decisive — a way to *measure* the forgetting rate, and the feature basis / learned correction operator that could trigger the framer's two escape hatches (the linear exception and the Newton-correct step).

## Measure the forgetting rate by feature-level causal tracing

The framer defines "forgets its past" operationally: a perturbation at time `t` is damped, not amplified, as it propagates forward, so a bad early guess is washed out (the harry lens restates this as "start two runs a hair apart and see whether they converge or drift apart"). Goodfire-style interp turns that prescription into a measurement at feature resolution. SAEs give a sparse, linear decomposition of each residual-stream state (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/), and causal tracing measures how much a single hidden state causally contributes to the final output: corrupt the input, restore one state to its clean value, and read the indirect effect on the output probability (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262). ROME runs this over 1,000 factual statements on GPT-2 XL (1.5B parameters) and finds the causal effect peaks at middle layers — e.g. an average indirect effect of 8.7% at layer 15 at the last subject token (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262). Sweeping the *lag* — perturb one feature (or state) at layer `t`, read the effect at `t+s` for increasing `s` — is precisely an empirical estimate of the framer's decay/Lyapunov exponent, computable even when the dynamics have no closed form. Feature clamping is the SAE-native version of the "hair apart" probe: clamping the Golden Gate Bridge feature (34M/31164353) to 10× its maximum activation changes downstream behaviour in a thematically consistent way, confirming the feature's forward influence (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/). This option does not restructure the computation; it is the diagnostic that decides whether any of the other options would converge.

## Linear feature representation -> parallel-in-time for free

The framer's §2.3 is the one clean exception: for a linear map `x_{t+1} = A x_t`, the final state is `x_T = A^T x_0`, computable in `O(log T)` depth by repeated squaring/diagonalisation (the ParaDiag/ParaExp family), with no forgetting assumption (https://www.unige.ch/~gander/siamtoc3.php). Goodfire-style interp supplies exactly the precondition for that exception: an SAE represents the residual stream as "an approximate decomposition of the model's activations into a linear combination of 'feature directions'" (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/). The linear representation hypothesis formalises the underlying idea — high-level concepts are represented as linear directions, and this is what connects probing (measurement) to steering (intervention), demonstrated on LLaMA-2 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658). If a trajectory is approximately linear in feature coordinates, the feature-to-feature transition matrix can be composed *out of order* — a feature-space ParaDiag — collapsing the sequential depth exactly as the framer's exception predicts. The open failure mode is visible in the same source: the 34M-feature SAE reconstructs only "at least 65% of the variance" of the activations (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/), so the residual nonlinearity is what would make an out-of-order composition drift from the true trajectory; whether the feature transition is time-invariant and context-stable enough to diagonalise is unmeasured.

## Feature steering as the guess-then-correct operator

The framer's proposal corrects a guessed trajectory with a Newton step `J^{-1}F`. Goodfire-style interp replaces that Newton solve with a learned, interpretable correction: feature steering "modif[ies] the model's internal state by adding a constant in the direction of that feature" (https://www.anthropic.com/research/evaluating-feature-steering), and the modified activation is reinserted and inference continues through the remaining layers (https://www.goodfire.com/research/understanding-and-steering-llama-3). A single clamped feature can reliably propagate through the whole remainder of generation — "Golden Gate Claude" steers nearly every answer toward the bridge from one feature clamp (https://www.anthropic.com/research/golden-gate-claude). The empirical limit on this correction is the analog of the framer's forgetting/contraction condition: Anthropic found a steering "sweet spot" of factors between −5 and +5 where behaviour changes without degrading capabilities, and "outside that sweet spot model capabilities degrade significantly" — i.e. a small correction propagates forward stably, a large one destabilises downstream, exactly as the framer predicts for contractive versus non-contractive corrections (https://www.anthropic.com/research/evaluating-feature-steering). This makes feature steering a candidate one-shot correction operator; what is not established is whether it can be composed as a *multiple-shooting* operator (a steering edit at several intermediate layers) that converges, which is the "correct every step at once" version of the proposal.

## Speculative and Jacobi decoding: the working guess-then-correct

The framer's abstract problem has a concrete, shipped solution on the one sequential computation mech interp can inspect: autoregressive LLM decoding, where "decoding K tokens takes K serial runs of the model" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192). The guess-whole-trajectory-then-correct realisation is speculative decoding — a small draft model guesses several tokens ahead, the target model verifies them in one parallel pass — giving a 2–3× wall-time speedup on T5-XXL with identical outputs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192); and Jacobi parallel decoding, which reframes greedy decoding as a fixed-point iteration, initialises the whole sequence, and refines every token in parallel until a fixed point, giving up to 38% speedup (nearly 2× on parallel resources) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10427). Both pay off exactly under the framer's forgetting condition: speculative decoding's speedup is governed by the expected acceptance rate `α = E[min(p(x), q(x))]`, the overlap between the draft and target distributions — i.e. how predictably the target's future tokens are determined by the current prefix, which is the forgetting rate (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192). Mech interp supplies the measurement of that gate: the tuned lens shows the model's latent prediction trajectory "converge[s] smoothly to the final output distribution" layer by layer (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112), which is the same "how predictable is the future state from the current one" quantity that sets `α` and decides whether parallel drafts are accepted.
